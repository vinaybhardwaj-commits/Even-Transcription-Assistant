## How to quote this result (eta-refuter #1991, reproduced from results_N.json; arithmetic re-checked)
Quote BOTH, not "no speech":
1. **Window bound, sensitivity-adjusted.** 0/30 detected bounds the share of refused windows that are speech-bearing BY NEMOTRON at 11.6 % (two-sided Clopper-Pearson). Nemotron found only 7 of 10 known faint-Indic positives, so the share that CONTAIN such speech is <= ~16.6 % at point sensitivity 0.70 (11.6 / 0.70) and <= ~33 % if the sensitivity's own 95 % lower bound (Clopper-Pearson 0.348 for 7/10) is used; ten positives leave the sensitivity wide.
2. **Per-second density contrast (the stronger evidence).** Nemotron speech density is 2 / 5,908 s = 0.034 % in the refused spans versus 309 / 842 s = 36.7 % in the Indic positives (about 1,000x lower); **[CORRECTED 25 Sep after eta-refuter #2067: the original sentence here said the 3 missed positives 'still contributed seconds'; that was FALSE, I had copied it without recomputing.]** Recomputed from results_N.json: the 3 positives Nemotron missed as windows gave 1.1 of 60 s, **0.0 of 60 s** and 11.6 of 89 s (pooled 12.7 / 209 s = 6.1 %, about 190 times the refused spans' 1.9 / 5,907.9 s = 0.032 %; all 10 positives pooled 308.9 / 842.0 s = 36.7 %, about 1,140 times). So the density contrast holds against faint speech ON AVERAGE, but because one real faint-Indic positive gave Nemotron zero seconds, per-second density alone cannot rule out that an individual refused window holds speech of that kind.
Reading with split-speaker's frozen arms and the fixed-window-fallback mechanism: the discarded spans are near-speech-free, within these bounds. This arm changes nothing in the frozen decision rule.
