Each item is one line of a transcript, exactly as a reader would see it, with the language the system claims for it. Play the audio (▶) to hear that line (~1 s of padding each side), read the text, then choose what kind of text it is. The English button shows a literal machine gloss when one exists - the first judgement is your read of the text. "Can't judge this language" skips the line without counting it. Tick any tags that fit and add a note freely; tags and notes never change your label. You are judging the transcript, not the doctor.

What the five labels mean

Readable: the line reads as real speech in the claimed language. Romanised readable: an Indic language written in Latin letters that a fluent reader can follow. Garbled: words that make no sense, endless repeats or invented text. Wrong language: the text is in a different language or script from the one claimed. Unsure: you cannot tell. Can't judge this language (skip): the line is in a language you cannot assess - it will not count. Your labels save as you go and only you see them here. On a computer, keys 1 to 6 choose the label, N or the right arrow moves on and P or the left arrow goes back.
